# Surface Archetypes

Choose the dominant archetype from the user's primary job. Mixed pages may contain secondary archetypes, but the first viewport needs one leader.

## Task-Led

Examples: login, signup, checkout, booking, search, upload, application, submission, recovery.

Protagonist: the task, its context, and current state.

Required regions:

- inputs, selectors, or provider actions;
- selected account, organization, product, appointment, or target when relevant;
- primary action and recovery path;
- error, denied, locked, loading, success, and retry states as appropriate;
- support, policy, device, session, deadline, or consequence context when useful.

Hard failures:

- a brand hero, illustration, or giant headline is visually louder than the task;
- a small form is attached to a marketing poster;
- a full-height divider creates two unrelated pages;
- required inputs or the primary action fall below `390x844` for a short task such as login;
- wide gutters carry only atmosphere while the form stays fixed and stranded.

Preferred wide models:

- integrated gateway: task plus compact organization/security context in one field;
- task with evidence rail: form remains primary, proof and support occupy a quieter adjacent region;
- scoped terminal: task, device/session state, and recovery share a command surface.

Mobile: brand becomes a compact header; task appears first; secondary evidence collapses below.

## Command-Led

Examples: dashboards, admin tools, monitors, editors, operational consoles.

Protagonist: current state plus available action.

Required regions: orientation, toolbar, filters, status, data/table/chart, selection, detail, feedback.

Hard failures:

- marketing hero or oversized slogan before the working surface;
- decorative cards replacing comparison or repeated operations;
- missing filters, selection, bulk actions, empty/loading/error states;
- low density that forces excessive scrolling for routine work.

Wide: let tables, timelines, charts, and details use width. Mobile: preserve the most important state and actions; move secondary detail into drawers or stacked regions.

## Inspection-Led

Examples: product, venue, book, portfolio work, real estate, profile, media.

Protagonist: the real object being inspected.

Required regions: high-quality media, metadata, variants/specifications, trust, availability, action.

Hard failures:

- fake object imagery or abstract placeholder used as proof;
- atmosphere prevents inspection;
- title and marketing copy outweigh the object;
- missing scale, state, price/access, provenance, or relevant metadata.

## Narrative-Led

Examples: campaign, cultural story, launch, editorial feature, expressive portfolio.

Protagonist: story, person, place, artwork, or message.

Allow stronger type, media, pacing, and motion, but preserve identity and continuation in the first viewport. A narrative page may spend more space on atmosphere; it may not use effects without a subject.

Hard failures:

- spectacle is interchangeable with another brand;
- the page depends on several generic motifs;
- motion has no causal or narrative role;
- the next path is hidden.

## Reading-Led

Examples: articles, documentation, reports, research, policies.

Protagonist: comprehension.

Required regions: title context, readable measure, hierarchy, navigation, citations/metadata, continuation.

Hard failures: oversized display type consuming reading space, decorative sidebars, weak contrast, poor line length, missing navigation for long content.

## Conversion-Led

Examples: landing pages, pricing, trial signup, lead generation.

Protagonist: a concrete offer supported by evidence.

Required path: offer, proof, action, risk/terms, comparison or detail when needed.

Hard failures: vague claim plus abstract artwork, fake metrics or logo walls, repeated feature cards, several competing CTAs, proof below excessive decoration.

## Pattern Selection Test

Complete this sentence:

`This composition serves [job] because [protagonist] appears first, [context/evidence] appears second, and [action/continuation] is reachable without hunting.`

If the sentence is weak or describes decoration instead of content, choose another composition.
